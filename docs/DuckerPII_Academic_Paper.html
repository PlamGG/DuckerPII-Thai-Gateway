Abstract
Deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) in enterprise conversational systems introduces severe data leakage risks under statutory privacy regulations such as Thailand’s Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA B.E. 2562). While neural token classifiers achieve high nominal in-domain accuracy, they exhibit severe shortcut memorization—failing catastrophically on unanchored chat messages, colloquial slang, and non-PII numeric tracking codes. In this paper, we present DuckerPII, a high-throughput, enterprise-grade privacy gateway featuring a Two-Tier Cascaded Hybrid Architecture. We introduce Thai-PII-Synthetic (v6.0.0), a 20,000-sentence corpus built exclusively on Privacy Enhancing Technologies (PETs) with zero real-world PII, incorporating 50% trigger-word dropout, 30% hard numeric negative injections, fragmented urban addresses, and cross-split disjoint template isolation. Tier 1 deploys DuckerPII-Thai-Base, a fine-tuned RoBERTa subword contextual classifier, while Tier 2 enforces deterministic mathematical checksum gates (Mod11 Thai ID Card), telecommunication prefix regexes, and RFC-compliant email validators. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that DuckerPII achieves a held-out Macro F1 of 0.9989 and an Out-of-Distribution (OOD) chat recall of 98.33%, reducing adversarial chat span leakage by 3x (from 5.00% to 1.67%) while delivering an ultra-low single-core CPU throughput of ~42 ms/sample (p50: 17.47 ms).
Keywords: Thai Named Entity Recognition, Personally Identifiable Information (PII), Synthetic Data, Mod11 Checksum, Privacy Enhancing Technologies, Adversarial Robustness.

1. Introduction

The proliferation of proprietary generative models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini 1.5) in enterprise applications has led to widespread transmission of unstructured customer conversations over public cloud APIs. In conversational commerce, customer service dialogues routinely contain sensitive Personally Identifiable Information (PII), including national identity numbers, bank accounts, home addresses, and telephone numbers.

Under statutory regulatory frameworks—such as the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and Thailand’s Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA B.E. 2562)—transmitting raw PII without explicit data processing agreements or rigorous de-identification induces severe regulatory penalties. In response, the Office of the Personal Data Protection Commission of Thailand (PDPC) issued the "Draft AI Privacy Guidelines (Version 1.0)", explicitly emphasizing Privacy Enhancing Technologies (PETs) and pre-inference log redaction.

However, Thai PII de-identification poses unique computational and linguistic challenges:

  1. Absence of Word Delimiters: Thai script is written without word boundaries, causing boundary slicing errors during tokenization.
  2. Shortcut Learning: Standard datasets condition models on explicit trigger markers (e.g., "ชื่อ:", "เบอร์:"), causing models to miss unanchored PII in informal chats.
  3. Numeric Ambiguity: Neural classifiers struggle to differentiate 13-digit ID cards, 10-digit phone numbers, tracking numbers (e.g., FLASH, Kerry), and inventory barcodes.

To resolve these bottlenecks, we introduce DuckerPII, a production-ready, cascaded hybrid framework combining deep representation learning with rigorous symbolic verification.

2. The Thai-PII-Synthetic (v6) Corpus

Training privacy models on scraped real data is inherently self-contradictory. To ensure strict PDPA compliance, the Thai-PII-Synthetic v6.0.0 corpus (20,000 instances; 60,677 entities) was synthesized algorithmically under four foundational invariants:

1. 50% Trigger Word Dropout: Anchoring phrases (e.g., "เบอร์ติดต่อ:", "จัดส่งที่:") are stochastically excised with probability p = 0.5, preventing superficial attention weight concentration.

2. Hard Numeric Traps (30% Injection): Non-PII numerical entities (e.g., order identifiers #ORD-918237, logistics tracking TH01928374, price tags, and inventory SKUs) are injected with target label O to teach explicit negative discrimination.

3. Fragmented Urban Topography: Real-world chats frequently omit formal administrative hierarchies (Subdistrict → District → Province). We inject short landmark references (e.g., "คอนโดเดอะเบส ชั้น 12 ตรงข้ามเซ็นทรัล") without postal codes.

4. Disjoint Split Architecture: Across Train (16k), Validation (2k), and Held-out Test (2k), name vocabularies and template grammar groups have 0% lexical overlap, guaranteeing zero benchmark memorization.

3. System Architecture

DuckerPII adopts a Two-Tier Cascaded Hybrid Architecture. Pure neural token classification is prone to subtle character hallucinations, whereas purely rule-based regex baseline achieves an unacceptable Address Recall of 0.00%. Our architecture cascades neural flexibility into symbolic rigor:

3.1 Tier 1: Neural Subword NER

The input string X = (c1, c2, ..., cN) is segmented via a SentencePiece subword tokenizer into tokens T = (t1, ..., tM). We deploy DuckerPII-Thai-Base, a 110M-parameter RoBERTa-based encoder with 12 hidden layers and 768 hidden dimensions:

H = RoBERTa(T) ∈ ℝM × 768
P(yi = c | T) = softmax(Wc hi + bc)

The model predicts token labels under an IOB2 sequence tagging scheme spanning 15 distinct classification states (7 entity categories × 2 plus O).

3.2 Tier 2: Deterministic Safety Gates

Candidate entity spans extracted by Tier 1 must pass through deterministic domain-specific verification filters before emission:

1. Mod11 ID Card Gate: Thai 13-digit National Identification numbers must satisfy the statutory weighted Modulo-11 checksum formula:

(∑i=112 di × (14 − i)) mod 11 = r
d13 = (11 − r) mod 10

Spans failing this mathematical invariant are demoted to ID_CARD_SUSPECTED or rejected.

2. Telecommunication Prefix Validator: Thai telephone numbers are filtered through official NBTC allocation prefixes: 

^(?:(?:\+66|0)[689]\d{8}|(?:\+66|0)2\d{7}|(?:\+66|0)[3-7]\d{7})$

3. RFC Fullmatch Email Filter: Eliminates false positive substring captures from non-email tracking codes (e.g., paid1234567890123).

4. Lexical Non-Alpha Filter: Strips leading honorific bugs and rejects misclassified numeric tokens from personal names.

5. Latency & Production SLA

Enterprise reverse proxies must guarantee sub-50ms latency SLAs. We benchmarked DuckerPII on single-core standard x86_64 CPU architecture:

  • p50 (Median Latency): 17.47 ms
  • p95 Latency: 38.12 ms
  • p99 Latency: 44.80 ms

The model’s compact footprint (~418MB safetensors) allows direct containerized deployment on serverless CPU instances (Docker/K8s) without requiring expensive dedicated GPU infrastructure.

6. Ethics & Responsible AI

In full alignment with the PDPC Draft AI Guidelines (Version 1.0) and NIST SP 800-122:

Exclusivity of Synthetic Data: No living individuals' data was processed or retained. All names and locations were generated via non-overlapping permutation cross-products.

Dual-Use Prevention: Generated numeric identifiers follow authentic grammatical syntax for detector training, but are stochastically unlinked from government civil registries. Unauthorized re-identification, surveillance, and automated phishing are strictly prohibited under the project license.

7. Conclusion

We presented DuckerPII, an end-to-end open-source suite for Thai PII de-identification. By pairing the synthetic data engineering of Thai-PII-Synthetic (v6.0.0) with a Two-Tier Cascaded Hybrid Architecture, DuckerPII bridges the gap between deep contextual comprehension and deterministic legal verification. The model weights, corpus, and interactive LINE simulator are publicly available to foster secure AI adoption in Thailand.
